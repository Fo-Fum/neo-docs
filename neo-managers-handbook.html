Neo Café Eatery Section One · Welcome

If you're reading this, you've been trusted with something significant. Not just a role — a responsibility. The responsibility of leading people.

That's not a small thing. Leadership done well changes the trajectory of a team. It shapes the experience our customers have, the culture our people work within, and the kind of business Neo becomes. We don't take it lightly, and we don't think you will either.

What We Believe About Leadership

Leadership at Neo has nothing to do with a title. It's about presence — showing up with intention, setting the standard, and creating the conditions for the people around you to do their best work.

Great leaders aren't the loudest voice in the room. They're the most consistent one. They keep sight of the bigger picture, even when the day gets busy, and the needs of the immediate get loud. They stay calm when the pressure rises. They notice what others miss — and they act.

That vantage point comes with responsibility: not to wield authority, but to exercise judgment. And good leadership, more than anything else, is good judgment exercised with care.

This Handbook
This isn't a rulebook. It's a compass. It's here to give you a clear picture of what leadership looks like at Neo — in practice, day to day — and what it asks of you. We hope you find it useful.
How We're Structured

Here we flip the traditional hierarchy on its head. Our job is not to direct from above, but to serve from below — clearing obstacles, creating clarity, and investing in the people around us so they can deliver brilliantly for our customers.

Notice that the structure balances on a point. When we lead poorly, it doesn't take long for the whole thing to wobble and fall. The stability of everything above us depends on the quality of what we do below.

Our Style: People-First Leadership

People-first leadership isn't soft leadership. It's a deliberate choice to put the growth and wellbeing of your team ahead of your own convenience, and sometimes your own comfort.

It means giving honest feedback even when it's hard. It means noticing when someone is struggling and stepping in before they have to ask. It means celebrating the wins of others as if they were your own.

But at its core, people-first leadership is about serving your team with the same care and intention we expect them to bring to our customers. Because hospitality starts with serving each other first.

At Neo, a great leader:
Sets the tone, pace, and standard — with warmth and gentle, consistent pressure
Communicates clearly, openly, and in a way that builds trust
Helps every team member understand how they contribute to the bigger picture
Spots potential early and actively supports people to grow
Challenges and coaches constructively — with honesty and respect
What You Bring

As a leader at Neo, you bring three things to everything you do:

01
Hands
Ready to contribute, pitch in, and do what's needed.
02
Mind
Switched on for problem-solving, creative thinking, and sound decisions.
03
Heart
Genuinely invested in the people and the mission.
Our Role and Use of Time

As a manager, you have to hold down a section and attend to the big picture — and it's not always easy to get that balance right. The day-to-day is important and essential, but it can't be allowed to crowd out the work that only you can do. Our highest-value contributions are creative and strategic: pushing the team forward, growing people, innovating our food and drink offering, and keeping the business moving in the right direction.

That work looks like this:

Setting direction and holding the big picture
Making the calls that only we can make
Solving problems at their root, not just their surface
Coaching and building team capability
Innovating and evolving our menu and drink offering
Think of it this way
Think of yourself as the oil in the engine. You permeate every part of the operation. You reduce friction. You enable others to do their best work. When you're doing your job well, everything runs smoothly — and most people won't even notice. That's the point.
We Are Problem Solvers
When we come up against an obstacle, our instinct is not to complain, escalate, or wait. We act — creatively, positively, and with ownership. There is almost always a way forward. Our job is to find it, or create it.

The Neo Team
One Team, One Dream

Neo has a kitchen. Neo has a floor. But Neo only has one team. In hospitality, it's easy for a divide to form between the kitchen and front of house. Different pressures, different rhythms, different worlds — and if left unattended, different agendas. That divide is one of the most common culture killers in any café or restaurant, and it's something we actively work against at Neo.

The customer doesn't experience "the kitchen" and "the floor" separately. They experience Neo. A cold dish, a long wait, a miscommunication between pass and table — whatever goes wrong, the customer just knows their experience fell short. It doesn't matter which side of the kitchen door the breakdown happened on. We all carry the result.

And when things go right — when the food is exceptional, the timing is seamless, the customer leaves glowing — that's everyone's win too. The barista, the chefs, the runner, the dishy. All of it, together. As a manager, you set the tone for how the two sides relate. Your job is to bridge the pass — literally and figuratively. Translate the pressure of the kitchen to the floor and the needs of the floor to the kitchen. Champion both. Blame neither.

In practice, this looks like:
Referring to the whole team as one — never "kitchen vs floor" in a way that implies opposition
Addressing tension between team members across both sides early and directly
Celebrating wins that involve both sides — calling out great teamwork across the pass
Making sure every team member understands how their contribution connects to the customer's experience
Never venting about one part of the team to the other

How We Grow Our People

As managers at Neo, our responsibility extends beyond the daily operation. We are investing in people — their character, their mindset, and their practical skills. We want every team member to discover their why, serve from the heart, and leave Neo more capable and more confident than when they arrived.

This means paying attention to who each person is becoming — not just how they perform. It means coaching with honesty and warmth, recognising growth as well as results, and creating an environment where people feel safe to stretch, fail, and try again.

The best outcome isn't just a well-run shift. It's a team member who walks out the door at the end of their time at Neo better equipped — in skill, in mindset, and in who they are — for whatever comes next in their life.

Exceptional Hospitality

Exceptional hospitality is the foundation on which everything at Neo is built. A thriving business, a motivated team, a positive impact on our community, a moment that changes someone's day — none of it happens without it. As a management team, our job is to create the conditions where exceptional hospitality can actually be delivered, every shift, every table.

Exceptional hospitality is the art of making people feel genuinely welcome, seen, and cared for — not just accommodated. It goes beyond meeting expectations to creating moments people remember.

The Principles of Exceptional Hospitality

At its deepest level, hospitality is an act of love. The great traditions — Greek xenia, Hebrew hesed, Japanese omotenashi — all understood it as something close to sacred: the meeting of the stranger with genuine care. These principles are Neo's expression of that same spirit.

Anticipation over reaction
Notice and meet needs before they're expressed. A glass of water before someone mentions thirst.
Presence and attentiveness
Actually pay attention to the person in front of you — their mood, their pace, what they leave unsaid.
Warmth without performance
Genuine hospitality feels effortless, not scripted. People sense the difference.
Dignity and respect
Every person is worthy of care — regardless of status, familiarity, or circumstance.
Personalisation
Remembering a name, a preference, something mentioned last time — these small acts say: you were truly received.
Graceful recovery
How a mistake is handled often matters more than the mistake itself.
Generosity of spirit
Great hospitality is generous by nature — a willingness to give time, attention, and care freely.

Our Values
How We Show Up

At Neo, values aren't aspirational language on a wall. They're the daily practice of how we lead, serve, and care for one another. As managers, we don't just endorse these values — we embody them. Culture is caught, not taught.

That also means holding people to account when they fall short. Not harshly — but clearly and promptly. Letting a values breach slide once signals that it's acceptable. Addressed well, it becomes one of the most powerful coaching moments you'll have.

1. Communication & Relationships

How we relate to one another — with honesty, equality, and the courage to say what needs to be said.

Communication. Clear is kind. We say what we mean, when it needs to be said, in a way that truly lands. We avoid mixed messages, assume good intent, and default to directness — always with care.
Equality. Every person who walks through our door — customer, team member, supplier, or courier — deserves the same level of respect. No hierarchy of kindness here.
Be Encouraging. We recognise people generously and often. We celebrate effort as much as outcome. Encouragement isn't a reward we save for exceptional moments — it's how we operate.
Difficult Conversations. We don't avoid the hard conversations — we approach them with curiosity and compassion. Handled well, they don't create conflict. They create growth.
2. Customer Experience

How we show up for the people we exist to serve — with warmth, care, and a genuine desire to exceed what they expected.

Customer Satisfaction. We aim for delight, not just adequate service. We're here to make every customer's experience easier, warmer, and more memorable than they expected.
Protecting the Relationship. When things go wrong — and sometimes they will — we own it immediately, fix it generously, and ensure the customer leaves feeling valued, not just compensated.
Exceeding Expectations. Fair pricing, exceptional quality, and standout service. That combination earns loyalty. We love the moment a customer realises we've gone further than they anticipated.

3. Integrity & Responsibility

How we operate — with honesty, high standards, and a commitment to doing what's right even when no one is watching.

Doing What's Right. The right decision and the easy decision are not always the same. At Neo, we choose integrity — every time, without exception.
Cleanliness & Presentation. A clean, welcoming environment is a non-negotiable expression of respect — for our customer, our food, and our team.
Efficiency. We look for smarter ways to work, not just harder ones. Continuous improvement isn't a project — it's part of how we think.
4. Growth & Development

How we invest in our people — building capability, learning from experience, and protecting the culture that makes Neo what it is.

Collective Growth. We are only as strong as the team around us. We invest in each other's development because your success is Neo's success.
Learning from Mistakes. We don't conceal or dwell on errors — we examine them honestly and move forward better. Mistakes are data. How we respond to them defines us.
Meaningful Contribution. The sweet spot is where individual passion meets business need. We work to help every team member find theirs.
The People We Hire. We recruit for character and train for skill. Culture is something we protect actively — by the standards we hold, the examples we set, and the people we welcome in.
Holding the Standard
Your team will not rise consistently above what they see modelled in front of them. The standard you set — and hold — is the culture you create.

Coaching & Giving Feedback

The best managers at Neo aren't just operators — they're developers of people. Coaching is not a formal event; it's a habit. A two-minute conversation after a shift, a quiet word at the pass, a thoughtful check-in mid-service.

The Principle
Feedback is a gift, not a weapon. It should be specific, timely, and delivered with the person's growth in mind — not your frustration.
In Practice — The SBI Framework
S
Situation
Describe the specific moment. "During the morning rush today..."
B
Behaviour
Name what you observed, not what you assumed. "I noticed you walked past the table three times without checking in..."
I
Impact
Explain the effect. "...the customer had finished eating and looked like they were waiting to leave."

Then invite a response. Ask what happened. Most of the time, a person who feels heard will course-correct themselves.

Positive feedback works the same way — and it matters just as much as correction. Recognition without specificity fades quickly. When you name exactly what someone did well and why it matters, you're not just making them feel good. You're showing them what to repeat.

On Positive Feedback
Specificity is what makes praise land. "Great job today" is forgotten by the car park. "The way you handled that complaint at table six — owning it immediately and making it right — that's exactly what Neo looks like at its best." That sticks.

Handling Pressure & Conflict

Pressure is permanent in hospitality. Conflict — between team members, with customers, within yourself — will come. The question is how you respond to it.

The Principle
You set the emotional temperature of the room. When you stay calm, your team stays calm. Composure under pressure is not a personality trait — it's a skill, and it's one you can build.
Conflict between team members
Address it privately and promptly
Listen to both sides before forming a view
Focus on behaviour and impact, not character
Agree on what changes, confirm clearly, and follow up
When you're under pressure
Slow your speech even if you're moving fast
Name the priority clearly
Delegate clearly — don't just hope
Debrief after the shift — that's where growth lives
Decision Making

Managers make dozens of decisions every shift — some small, some significant. The goal isn’t perfection — it’s sound judgment, made quickly enough to keep the operation moving.

The Principle
A good decision made promptly is almost always better than a perfect decision made too late. Indecision has a cost too — it creates uncertainty, slows the team, and signals a lack of confidence.
In Practice — A Simple Decision Filter

When you’re unsure how to call something, run it through three questions:

Is this right for the customer?
Does this decision serve the experience we're trying to create?
Is this right for the team?
Does it set a fair and consistent precedent?
Is this right for the business?
Is it sustainable and does it reflect our values?

If the answer to all three is yes, move. If one is no, pause and think. If you can't resolve it, check your thinking with someone else — remember you're not alone in this.

Own your decisions. When they're right, share the credit. When they're wrong, say so clearly, learn from it, and move on. A manager who can't admit a mistake loses their team's trust faster than almost anything else.

Goal Setting & Performance

People do their best work when they know what they're working towards and feel that someone is fully invested in their progress. Goal setting at Neo isn't a HR exercise — it's one of the most practical leadership tools you have.

Remember our People Vision: to build a team who grow in character, skill, confidence, and resilience. That's what coaching and goal-setting are in service of — not just better shifts, but better people. When you invest in someone's growth, you're honouring that vision in the most direct way possible.

The Principle
Clarity is kindness. Vague expectations produce vague results. When people know exactly what good looks like — and feel supported in reaching it — most of them will rise to it.
Setting goals with your team
1
Make it specific and observable
Not "improve your service" — but "know the name of at least five regulars by the end of the month."
2
Connect it to the bigger picture
Help each person see how their growth contributes to Neo's mission.
3
Set a timeframe — and check in early
Check in before the deadline, not only at it. Early support changes outcomes.
4
Celebrate progress, not just outcomes
The effort to grow deserves recognition even when the goal isn't fully reached yet.
For underperformance

Address it early. A small gap is easy to close. Left unspoken, it becomes a pattern — and patterns are much harder to shift. When you do address it, be specific about the standard, clear about what needs to change, and honest about the consequences if it doesn't. Then give the person a legitimate chance to respond and improve.

If meaningful improvement doesn't follow after clear coaching and a fair timeframe, that's the point to escalate. Bring the conversation to your senior manager or the owner — with your notes, your observations, and the support you've already offered. You're not failing the team member by escalating. You're making sure they receive the right level of support — and that the team around them is protected.

A Final Word
You're Not Alone in This
This is the Standard —
and It's Worth It.

Leadership is one of the most stretching, rewarding, and occasionally humbling things a person can take on. You've chosen to show up for something bigger than yourself — for a team, a culture, a place that genuinely matters to people. That choice deserves to be honoured. And we want you to know: you don't carry it alone.

What's described in these pages is a high bar. Servant leadership, exceptional hospitality, intentional culture — none of it happens by accident, and none of it is easy to sustain. There will be shifts where it all clicks, and shifts where it doesn't. Both are part of the job. What we ask is not perfection. We ask for consistency, integrity, and care.

We ask that you keep returning to the mission and the values — not as a checklist, but as a compass. And we ask that you be patient with yourself and your team as the standard is built, tested, and rebuilt. The standard is worth holding because the people are worth it. Your team are worth it. Your customers are worth it. And you are worth the investment of becoming the kind of leader this place calls you to be.

On the hard days — when the team is tired, the kitchen is behind, and nothing seems to be going right — come back to why we're here. To create special moments. To build community. To make people feel truly welcome.

Look After Yourself Too

Leadership at its best is sustainable. If you're running on empty, struggling with the pressure, or losing sight of why you're here — say something. Talk to a fellow manager, talk to the owner. We invest in our team members' growth and wellbeing. That includes yours.

Keep Coming Back to the Mission

That mission doesn't change with the season or the staffing levels. It's the thing that holds everything else together. When you lead from it — when your team can feel it in how you speak and how you act — it becomes contagious. That's how culture gets built. Not in one big moment, but in hundreds of small ones, every single day.

So lead with your hands, your mind, and your heart. Set the tone. Hold the standard. Take care of your people. And trust that when you do — consistently, generously, and with intention — something remarkable happens in this place.

Welcome to the team. We're glad you're here.

— The Neo Leadership Team
